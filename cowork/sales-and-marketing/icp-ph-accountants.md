# ICP — Philippine Accountants (Primary B2B Channel Partners)

**Primary target for the avintph.com motion.** The buyer is the accountant; the end users are the accountant's small-business and freelancer clients. The play is a partnership: accountants recommend AVIntelligence to their clients for receipt and document organization, which makes the accountant's monthly bookkeeping work faster and gives them a defensible audit trail.

## Definition

**In-scope:**
- PH-based CPAs running solo or 2–8 person practices
- PH-based unlicensed bookkeepers serving SMBs / sole proprietors / freelancers
- Outsourced accounting service providers serving PH-based businesses
- Virtual / cloud-first PH accounting practices
- PICPA-affiliated practitioners offering bookkeeping + tax compliance services

**Out of scope (do not contact):**
- Big 4 PH offices (SGV/EY, Isla Lipana/PwC, R.G. Manabat/KPMG, Navarro Amper/Deloitte) — wrong tier
- Audit-only practices (no monthly bookkeeping work)
- Tax compliance services that don't touch source documents
- Practices serving listed corporations or BSP-supervised entities (wrong fit)
- Non-PH-based accountants serving PH clients only via remote portals (revisit later)

## The Partnership Premise

We give the accountant something they can hand to their client. Their client uses AVIntelligence to upload receipts, contracts, payslips, invoices — the system organizes them and produces reports. The accountant then works from cleaner inputs instead of chasing the client for missing or messy documents.

This is the wedge. It's not "switch your books to us." It's "your client uses us upstream; you get a better Friday."

## Pain Points (in priority order)

1. **Receipt and document chasing from clients** — clients send receipts late, blurry, sa Viber, sa email, sa Messenger, halu-halo. Reconciling takes hours per client per month.
2. **Inconsistent client document quality** — some clients are organized, some aren't; the accountant absorbs the variance.
3. **Month-end and BIR-deadline crunches** — receipts and books pile up against compliance dates (1601-C/E, 2550M/Q, 1701/1702, 0619, etc.).
4. **Time-per-client cap on practice growth** — each client demands fixed hours of manual cleanup; more clients = more hours = bottleneck on FTE capacity.
5. **Looking competent during BIR audit / LOA encounters** — a clean, indexed source trail makes the difference between a smooth resolution and a panic week.
6. **Client retention** — when a client's books are a mess and the accountant is constantly nagging, the relationship strains. A client-facing tool that makes the client look organized helps both sides.

## What They Care About (in priority order)

1. **Reduced manual work per client** (more clients per FTE = direct revenue)
2. **Looking professional to clients and the BIR**
3. **Tools their clients will actually use** (low onboarding friction is critical — most PH SMB clients are not technical)
4. **Source-document audit trail** — defensible against BIR, defensible against the client if questions arise
5. **A reason to be the "modern" accountant in their client's eyes** — differentiation against legacy practices
6. **Cost** — matters, but rarely the deciding factor; the time saved is the real currency

## What Makes Them Recommend Us

Accountants recommend tools that:
- Reduce work the accountant does (not just the client)
- Make the client look organized when they shouldn't be
- Don't threaten the accountant's role (we are explicitly NOT bookkeeping software)
- Don't require the accountant to learn or maintain a new tool themselves
- Don't cost the client so much that the client cancels the accountant relationship to afford it
- Have a clear, audit-friendly output (the accountant can show it to a reviewer)

## The Trojan-Horse Frame (positioning to accountants)

When pitching the partnership, the right frame is:

> "We're not replacing what you do. We're handling the part you hate. Your client gets a place to drop receipts and a clean monthly summary. You get to do the actual accounting work instead of chasing documents. You look like the modern firm. We don't talk to the BIR; you do."

This frame:
- Respects the accountant's professional role (legally, technically, relationally)
- Avoids triggering the "this tool will replace me" fear
- Makes the accountant the hero in their client relationship
- Positions us as infrastructure, not competition

If a draft drifts off this frame (toward "replace your accounting tool" or "we do tax prep"), it gets rejected.

## Local-flavor Phrasing Rules (per voice-and-tone.md)

When drafting to PH accountants:

- **Default to English.** PH professional communication leans English; do not surprise the recipient with Filipino unless we have evidence they communicate that way publicly.
- **Light Filipino is OK in social/community contexts** if the recipient's posts show that voice (e.g., quoted phrases like "nag-Viber ng resibo" in a relatable hook are acceptable; full Filipino paragraphs are not, unless the founder explicitly opts in).
- **Avoid US-only references** (Schedule C, Form 1099, TurboTax) when the audience is PH-only. Use PH equivalents (BIR Form 2316, 1701, eFPS) when those genuinely apply.
- **Don't pretend to be from the same university or organization** as the recipient unless that's factually true.
- **Don't claim PH-government certification** (BIR, SEC, BSP, etc.) — we don't have it.
