Primary acquisition target for the US Schedule C / Extension Rescue line. Buyer has the pain, the budget, and the downstream client portfolio.

## Definition

**In-scope:**
- Solo bookkeepers (1 person, US-based, serves SMBs / freelancers / sole props)
- 2–5 person bookkeeping practices
- 1–3 person CPA practices that do bookkeeping + tax prep
- "Virtual bookkeepers" / cloud-first practices

**Out of scope (do not contact):**
- Big 4 / regional CPA firms
- Audit-only practices (no bookkeeping work)
- Tax-prep-only practices (no monthly client work — wrong timing for our value)
- Bookkeepers serving >$50M revenue clients (different tooling needs)
- Outside the US (currency + Schedule C don't apply)

## Pain Points (in priority order)

1. **Receipt-chasing from clients** — clients send receipts late, blurry, or in 12 different formats. Bookkeeper spends hours organizing before they can categorize.
2. **Year-end / tax-season cleanup** — January–April is brutal because clients dump 12 months of mess at once.
3. **1099 contractor tracking** — figuring out which payments to which contractors hit the $600 threshold.
4. **Client communication overhead** — chasing missing docs, clarifying ambiguous transactions.
5. **Looking competent under deadline pressure** — if their client's CPA complains about messy books, the bookkeeper loses the client.

## What They Care About (in priority order)

1. **Time saved per client** (more clients per FTE = direct revenue)
2. **Looking professional to their clients and to downstream CPAs**
3. **Tools their clients can use without complaining** (low onboarding friction)
4. **Defensible audit trail** (not getting blamed when something is wrong)
5. **Cost** (matters, but #4 in their priority — they bill clients hourly)

## Trigger Events (high-priority outreach moments)

- Posted about onboarding a new client this week
- Posted complaint about manual receipt work
- Posted about scaling / hiring / capacity
- Posted about client communication tools
- Posted in a "what tools do you use" thread

## Disqualifiers (move on, don't contact)

- Bio mentions "we use [competing all-in-one tool] for everything"
- Practice URL shows enterprise client list (Big Co logos)
- No US presence
- Last post >12 months ago (inactive practice)
- Personal-only profile, no practice mentioned

## Hook Templates

Prefer the most specific hook the data supports. Generic hooks = weak leads.

1. **Recent post hook:** "Saw your post in [community] about [specific thing] on [date] — [observation]"
2. **Practice profile hook:** "Looked at [practice URL] — you focus on [niche]. The receipt-org pain is sharpest there because [reason]."
3. **Tool gap hook:** "You mention [tool X] in your stack — most [tool X] users still hand-categorize receipts. We bridge that step."
4. **Client mix hook:** "Your client mix skews [freelancers/sole props/creative agencies]. Schedule C density there is [observation]."

## Pricing Anchors for Outreach

Don't lead with price. If asked:
- DFY service for their clients: **$99–$249/client** (white-label optional)
- Pro tier (their own AVInt account): **standard pricing**
- Bulk / referral deals: **negotiable after first 3 client wins**

## What NOT to Promise

- Not a bookkeeping platform (no GL, no reconciliation, no payroll)
- Not a tax-prep tool
- Not a QuickBooks replacement
- Not "instant" — service delivery takes 5 business days
