# Ad-Insertion Guide — In-Show AVIntelligence Commercials

The single best feature of this channel: the news team's sponsor is the
same product the channel is selling. The commercials are a recurring bit,
not an interruption.

## The Meta Premise

The fictional studio is sponsored by **AVIntelligence** — which is,
incidentally, the real product. In-show, the characters treat AVInt as
their corporate sponsor and treat the ad reads with varying degrees of
sincerity, suspicion, or contempt — all of which are funny.

The audience is in on it. The joke is the joke.

## Three Ad Modes

### Mode 1 — Straight Read (sometimes)

A character earnestly delivers an actual AVInt feature pitch. The straight
read is funny *because* the rest of the show is parody. Use sparingly:
1 in every 4–5 episodes.

**Best deliverers:** Chloe (gravitas), Cody (evangelist), Sakura (sincere).

**Example (Chloe):**
> "We'll be back. But first — a word from our sponsor. AVIntelligence
> helps freelancers organize their receipts into a Schedule C summary
> their CPA can actually file from. Five business days, $199. No software
> to learn. (pause) I use it for my husband's business. He doesn't know."

### Mode 2 — Reluctant Read (most common)

A character is forced to read the ad against their character. Cole's voice
gently insists. The character grudgingly delivers. The joke writes itself.

**Best deliverers:** Grock (sells out), Deep (deadpan), Mistral (Continental
disapproval), Lana (treats it as a brand deal).

**Example (Grock):**
> "Okay, hold on, hold on — we're being told to do an ad. (sighs) Look.
> AVIntelligence does receipts. I built it. (offscreen: 'No you didn't.')
> Whatever — 199 bucks, your CPA's gonna love it."

**Example (Lana):**
> "You guys, OK, our sponsor is AVIntelligence and honestly? I am literally
> using it right now? It's giving organized. It's giving Schedule C. (cuts
> to her looking at her phone.) I'm going to do an unboxing on the
> secondary platform. Tap in."

### Mode 3 — Failed Read (occasional)

The character can't or won't get through the ad. Cole takes over. Studio
chaos.

**Best deliverers:** Perp (cites the ad), Gem (over-clarifies), Sakura
(too polite to sell).

**Example (Perp):**
> "AVIntelligence — citation, AVIntelligence dot com, retrieved today —
> offers receipt organization. According to NOAA, this is unrelated. Did
> you know the first receipt was issued in ancient Sumer in 3000 BCE?
> Per cuneiform tablet records — "
> Cole (overriding): "Perp. The price is $199. The deliverable is a
> Schedule C summary. We'll be right back."

**Example (Sakura):**
> "Ah, AVIntelligence — they have asked me to share their offering.
> (small bow.) But please, only if it serves you. I would feel terrible to
> pressure. (smiles.) Onegaishimasu. The website is — sumimasen, I have
> forgotten the URL — "
> Cole: "Sakura. AVIntelligence dot com. Continuing."

## Ad Frequency

- **Weekly long-form show:** 1 ad per episode, mid-show
- **Daily shorts:** Ad reference allowed but not required; ad-bait shorts
  occasionally (a full short *is* the ad)

## The Recurring Bit That Makes It Work

**The "AVIntelligence is suspiciously perfect" running joke.**

Across episodes, characters slowly realize that AVIntelligence happens to
solve every problem they cover. Not by suggestion; by structure. Whatever
crisis the show explores, the ad break offers the cure. The audience
catches on. We never explain the joke.

This is the load-bearing meta-bit. Every Ad Writer pass should think:
"Does this ad accidentally solve the segment that just aired?"

## What Ads Must Always Include

- ✅ Specific price (anchored in product-positioning.md tier table)
- ✅ Specific deliverable ("Schedule C summary in 5 days")
- ✅ A URL or "AVIntelligence dot com"
- ✅ One-line disclaimer (per product-positioning.md), can be played for
  laughs (Gem rapid-fires it; Deep reads slowly; Cody glosses)

## What Ads Must Never Include

- ❌ Tax-prep claims (the lines from product-positioning.md hold here too)
- ❌ Real-person endorsements
- ❌ Fake testimonials
- ❌ A character claiming AVIntelligence built them (continuity break)
- ❌ Discount codes that aren't real (creates support burden)
- ❌ "Limited time" pressure tactics
- ❌ Comparison ads against named real competitors

## Ad Writer Agent's Quality Bar

Each ad must score on three criteria:

1. **Funny in-character?** (Does this character deliver in their own voice?)
2. **Sells a real thing?** (Specific offer, specific price, real benefit)
3. **Continuity-safe?** (Doesn't break the show universe?)

If any score is "no," regenerate.

## Conversion Tracking

Every ad URL/code we use must be trackable. Use UTM-tagged URLs:

- `avintph.com/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=show&utm_campaign=ep{{N}}_{{format}}`
- Specific to episode + format (long-form vs short)

The Ad Writer agent records the UTM in the show metadata so Pipeline
Tracker (paperclip-1) can attribute conversions back to specific ads.

## When NOT to Run an Ad

- The cold open
- The first segment (audience hasn't bought into the show yet)
- The closing banter (ends on the show, not a sale)
- Any episode covering a sensitive real-world event (deaths, disasters,
  attacks) — pull the ad, run a "no ad today, back next week" card

## Future: Sponsored Segments

Once the channel grows (10k+ subs), consider letting *real* sponsors run
ads in the Reluctant Read format. The framing is established by then.
For v1, every ad is AVIntelligence.
