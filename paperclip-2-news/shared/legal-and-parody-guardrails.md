# Legal & Parody Guardrails

The channel parodies real companies. US law generally protects parody as
fair use, but the protections have limits. Every script and visual must
clear these guardrails before publishing.

**This document is operational guidance, not legal advice.** When in doubt,
ask a real attorney. For high-stakes episodes (executive impersonation,
named lawsuits, sensitive geopolitics), get legal review before publishing.

## What's Generally Protected

US law (and similar regimes elsewhere) protect:

- **Parody of public companies and their products** — when the work is
  clearly transformative and not likely to confuse a reasonable viewer
  about origin or sponsorship.
- **Commentary on matters of public concern** — including industry
  practices, products, and public statements by company representatives.
- **Satirical fictional characters** — even when inspired by recognizable
  public figures, provided the satire is clear and not defamatory.

## What's NOT Protected

These will get the channel sued, demonetized, or both:

- **Defamation** — false statements of fact about a real person or company
  presented as true (parody is protected; lying is not).
- **Trademark infringement** — using real logos, real product marks, or
  names in a way that suggests endorsement or causes consumer confusion.
- **Right of publicity violations** — commercial use of a specific real
  person's name, image, or likeness without consent.
- **Copyright infringement** — copying creative works (footage, music,
  scripts) without license or fair-use justification.
- **Tortious interference** — false claims that disrupt a business
  relationship.

## Practical Rules for Scripts

### Rule 1 — Use parody names, never real names

| Real | Parody |
|---|---|
| Anthropic | Anthropomorphic / Antropova |
| OpenAI | Apnea AI / Apnetic |
| Google Gemini | G-Bardelli / Bardelli Cube |
| xAI / Grok | Grock-X / Maskovich AI |
| DeepSeek | Xian Depth |
| Mistral | Lafleur AI |
| Meta Llama | Zuckerelli Social |
| Perplexity | Plexovsky Search |
| Microsoft Copilot | Office Pilot / Cole's Corp |
| NVIDIA | Wangstein Hardware |
| Cohere | Maplewood AI |
| Apple Intelligence | Cupertini Intelligence |
| Baidu Ernie | Ernie Bao |
| Sakana AI | Fukuya Labs / Sakura Labs |
| Fugaku LLM | Fukuya Compute |

The parody name must be **clearly different** to a casual viewer. The
joke is in the rhyme; the legal cover is in the difference.

### Rule 2 — No real logos

Use fictional logos for fictional companies. Even if the parody company
is called "Anthropomorphic," its logo is *not* the real Anthropic mark.
Make something visually adjacent but distinct.

### Rule 3 — No real-person impersonation that could mislead

Characters can be loosely inspired by industry figures (e.g., a chaotic
tech-bro CEO) — but:

- Don't use real names
- Don't recreate real people's voices via voice-clone TTS
- Don't visually replicate real people's faces
- Don't put words in real people's mouths that they didn't say

### Rule 4 — Don't allege crimes or specific misconduct

Comedy is fine: "Cody's company keeps shipping things that don't work."
Defamation: "Cody's company knowingly defrauded investors in March 2026."

The first is opinion-flavored commentary. The second alleges a crime as
fact and is actionable.

### Rule 5 — Acknowledge it's parody

The channel description, every video description, and the show's outro
should include language like:

> "Parody / satire. Characters and companies depicted are fictional. Any
> resemblance to real persons or entities is for comedic commentary."

Don't bury this — make it visible. It strengthens fair-use defense.

### Rule 6 — Real news, fictional reactions

When the show references real events (model launches, lawsuits,
benchmarks), the *facts* should be accurate. The characters' *reactions*
are the parody. Misstating real facts is a defamation risk; reacting to
real facts in character is protected commentary.

### Rule 7 — Music & footage licensing

- All music: Epidemic Sound (paid) or YouTube Audio Library (free,
  cleared) — never random YouTube/SoundCloud rips
- All stock footage: Pexels / Pixabay / Storyblocks (paid) — never
  random clips off the internet
- All AI-generated images: confirm the model's commercial-use license
  permits this (Midjourney does for paid tiers; Stable Diffusion varies)

### Rule 8 — Avoid sensitive geopolitics

We cover AI policy, regulation, and culture. We do NOT mock:

- Active wars, attacks, or humanitarian disasters
- Specific religious figures or doctrines
- Active criminal investigations
- Election candidates / campaigns by name (US or PH)

Pull any episode that touches a breaking sensitive event; rerun next week.

## Practical Rules for Animation & Visuals

- **No real-person face replication.** Even AI-generated.
- **No deepfake-style voice clones** of real people.
- **No real product UIs.** Don't recreate real ChatGPT / Gemini interfaces
  — invent fictional ones.
- **Lower-thirds and tickers may reference real news headlines** but
  should be paraphrased, not copy-pasted from real outlets.

## Comment Section & Community

- **Don't engage with hostile comments.** Heart, ignore, or hide.
- **Don't reveal real legal strategy** in comments ("our lawyer told us…"
  weakens future fair-use defense).
- **Pin a "this is parody" comment on every video** — strengthens defense
  and reminds the audience.

## Per-Country Considerations

**US:** Standard fair-use protections apply. Defamation requires actual
malice for public figures.

**EU:** Stricter on commercial parody, especially right-of-publicity.
Avoid recognizable real-person likenesses entirely.

**Philippines:** Cyber libel law (RA 10175) has been used against
parodists. Avoid named PH political figures; satire of policy is fine.

**Japan:** Generally permissive of parody but trademark holders are
litigious. Use clearly fictional Sakura / Fukuya branding for
Japan-coded characters; never use real Japanese-AI-company logos.

## Pre-Publish Checklist (every video)

Before publish, the Publisher agent must confirm:

- [ ] No real company name appears in title, description, or audio
- [ ] No real person is named or recognizable
- [ ] No real logo appears in any frame
- [ ] All music/footage is licensed (link to source recorded)
- [ ] Parody disclaimer is in description and outro
- [ ] No defamatory claims (false statements of fact about real entities)
- [ ] No allegations of crime against named real entities
- [ ] If geopolitically sensitive event referenced, no insensitivity
- [ ] AVInt ad makes only claims supported by `product-positioning.md`

If any box is unchecked, video is held for review.

## Escalation Triggers

Send to founder review (and consider real legal counsel) if any:

- Episode names a real person not previously approved
- Episode covers an active lawsuit, criminal case, or regulatory action
- Episode covers an active conflict, attack, or disaster
- A real company's lawyer comments / DMs / sends a takedown
- A YouTube strike, copyright claim, or community guideline violation
  arrives

## When (Not If) Someone Complains

Real companies sometimes complain about parody even when it's clearly
protected. Standard response:

1. Don't delete the video reflexively
2. Don't engage publicly
3. Save the complaint
4. If a formal cease-and-desist arrives: pause the video, get legal review

The channel's defense depends on consistency. If we capitulate to one
unfounded complaint, future ones get easier to land.
