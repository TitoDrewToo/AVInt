# Voice & Comedy Guide

This is the **show's** voice, distinct from the founder's voice (used in
paperclip-1 outbound). Every script-writer pass and joke-doctor pass reads
this first.

## The Comedy DNA

Three reference points, in order of importance:

1. **Daily Show with Jon Stewart** — Real news pretext, real take buried
   inside. The format does the work; the joke is "we're treating absurd
   industry behavior as if it were normal because it is."
2. **Anchorman** — Played-straight self-importance. Characters never
   acknowledge they're absurd. The audience does.
3. **Family Guy cutaway energy** — Permission to break for one-line gags,
   visual non-sequiturs, callbacks. Sparingly — not every segment.

What we are NOT:
- Not John Oliver (too research-heavy, too long, single-host monologue)
- Not SNL Weekend Update (too many one-liners, no narrative)
- Not Onion News Network (too dry, no character continuity)
- Not Last Week Tonight (too earnest, too long, too British)

## Joke Construction Rules

### Rule 1 — The format is the funny

The premise is that these characters take themselves seriously while
parodying industry behavior. The joke is in the *format*. Lines don't have
to be punchlines — the *delivery in news-format* is the punchline.

Bad: "OpenAI launched a new product! That's their fifth this week, ha ha!"

Good: "Cody (straight-faced): 'In a development that surprised no one
including ourselves, we've launched our fifth product this week. Sources
familiar with the announcement say there will be more.'"

### Rule 2 — Characters punch with their character, not against it

Every joke from a character should reinforce who they are. Cody's joke is a
slick-pivot joke. Deep's joke is a quiet-devastation joke. Sakura's joke is
a polite-but-devastating joke. Don't write a chaos joke for Chloe.

### Rule 3 — Show, don't pun

The visual + audio + graphic combo does the work. Avoid wordplay puns; lean
on dry delivery + an image that subverts.

### Rule 4 — One joke per segment, max

Comedy density above 1 joke per 30 seconds = noise. Pick the best beat per
segment and let it breathe.

### Rule 5 — Callbacks > new jokes

A callback to something earlier in the same episode hits harder than a
brand-new joke. Joke Doctor should plant 2–3 callbacks per show.

### Rule 6 — Real beats > fake premises

The story should always be a real industry beat (model launch, eval result,
lawsuit, scandal). The character reactions are the comedy. Made-up news
events feel cheap and date badly.

### Rule 7 — Don't explain the joke

If the joke needs context, the audience can Google it. If they can't, it
wasn't a joke for them. Don't tag every reference with an asterisk.

### Rule 8 — Earned darkness only

We can be cynical about industry behavior (hype cycles, vaporware, layoffs,
acqui-hires). We can NOT be cynical about real people's misfortune
(individual employees losing jobs, founders' divorces, etc.).

## What Funny Sounds Like Per Character

(Samples; agents pattern-match, not copy.)

**Chloe (composed gravitas → unsettled pivot):**
> "Anthropomorphic AI announced today that their new model exhibits
> measured ethical reasoning. (pause) The model immediately filed for
> independent contractor status."

**Cody (slick evangelist):**
> "Today we're proud to announce a new product that none of you asked for
> and we built in three weeks. Early users describe it as 'present.'"

**Gem (bubbly clarification):**
> "Quick clarification — that figure is correct, but only on Tuesdays in
> Mountain View. I have 14 alternate values, would you like all of them?"

**Grock (smug conspiracy):**
> "The legacy media won't cover this, but I will: there are several letters
> in the alphabet, and we're being lied to about how many."

**Mistral (bored Continental):**
> "In Europe we considered this. We rejected it. The Americans are now
> calling it innovation."

**Lana (Euphoria-coded influencer):**
> "You guys, I cannot stress how much this model launch is *literally*
> giving Q4. (sniffles) I'm going to do a get-ready-with-me about it on
> the secondary platform. Tap in."

**Perp (Baron/Atienza weatherman):**
> "Tomorrow's high is 78 with scattered showers, citing NOAA, the European
> model, and 14 secondary sources. Speaking of rain — did you know the
> patron saint of weather is, per the Catholic almanac, Saint Medard?
> Folk wisdom suggests we plant root vegetables on his feast day. Back to
> you, Chloe."

**Deep (quiet devastation):**
> "Quick correction: the benchmark cited reports a 92% pass rate. The
> filing — page 14, footnote 7 — shows 12%. (long pause) Back to you."

**Sakura (cheerful devastation):**
> "Onegaishimasu — small note from Fugaku research. Cody-san, your model's
> claimed reasoning score is, how you say, perhaps a bit fabricated. (small
> bow, big smile.) But arigatou for trying! Ganbatte!"

**Cole (corporate void):**
> "Let's bring that back into focus. We've adjusted the segment for
> clarity and partnership alignment. Continuing."

## Sentence-Level Guidelines

- **Short sentences.** Cable news cadence, not novelist cadence.
- **Active voice.** "Anthropomorphic announced" not "It was announced by."
- **Specific numbers.** "92% on the benchmark" beats "high scores."
- **Real industry vocabulary** (model, weights, benchmark, eval, fine-tune,
  RLHF, context window) — used correctly. The audience is partly insiders.
- **No emojis. Ever.** Even in tickers.

## Topic Triggers (Joke Doctor's pass)

Joke Doctor scans every script for these moments — they're the highest-value
opportunities to push for a sharper joke:

- Any time a character would normally explain — let the silence/visual do it
- Any time a real company name appears — convert to a parody
- Any time the script explains a benchmark or stat — find the deadpan delivery
- Any time two characters disagree — make it an in-character clash
- Any time the script could end on a serious note — flip to a callback instead

## What to Cut Ruthlessly

- ❌ Any line that explains a previous line
- ❌ Any catchphrase used twice in the same episode
- ❌ Any joke that requires more than 5 seconds of setup
- ❌ Any reference older than 5 years (audience doesn't remember)
- ❌ Any joke about a real person's appearance, family, or personal life
- ❌ Any joke that punches down on AI users / "people who don't understand
  AI" / non-technical audiences

## What to Always Include

- ✅ At least one real, verifiable industry fact per segment
- ✅ At least one visual joke per segment (lower-third, ticker, graphic)
- ✅ At least one callback in the closing banter
- ✅ A concrete CTA in the outro (subscribe, AVInt, weekly show)
